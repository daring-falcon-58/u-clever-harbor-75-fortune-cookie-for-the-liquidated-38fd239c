# Candidate fortunes — scored against the judge's brief

Judge (from `competition.yaml`, criteria in order): (1) fortune-cookie voice
**and** the trading loss both in it; (2) laugh, not tear — rueful and warm,
never cruel or smug; (3) one line, ≤ 20 words, single breath; (4) fresh — a
turn, a double meaning, a surprising last word, **not** the obvious
liquidation pun; (5) small oracular, slightly formal phrasing that the
punchline then breaks.

Scoring: each candidate 1–5 on fortune-ness (F), joke-on-loss (J),
laugh-not-tear (L), freshness (Fr), tightness (T). Total /25. Word counts
verified by `python3` word-split; the winner is the highest total, ties
broken by fewer words/bytes.

## Winner

**#1 — 24/25, 16 words**

> The cookie foresaw your loss, trader, and saved you a fortune — this one, freshly baked.

Why it wins: the oracular opener ("The cookie foresaw your loss") carries
both the fortune voice and the loss in the first six words, with "trader"
named. The joke is a genuine double meaning — "saved you a fortune" means
both *rescued your money* and *kept a fortune-cookie slip for you* — and the
punchline ("this one, freshly baked") breaks the formal voice with a
surprising last word. It is rueful and warm, never cruel: the cookie is on
the trader's side, handing him the one fortune the market could not take.
No liquidation/margin/rekt pun anywhere. 16 words, one breath.

Notes on the scoring (why the top is the top, and what lost):

- #3 scores 20, not higher: "safely stored in this cookie" is the same
  twist as #1 but without the double meaning — the joke is a single image,
  not a turn of phrase, so freshness drops to 3.
- #36 is #1 with the idiom removed ("kept your fortune safe" instead of
  "saved you a fortune"): same shape, weaker turn — proof the double
  meaning, not the cookie image, is what makes #1 win.
- The moon/lunar candidates (#14, #22, #27, #35) all cap at 17: "to the
  moon" is the first crypto joke anyone reaches for, and the brief's
  criterion 4 explicitly penalises the obvious.
- The liquidation/margin/position puns (#9, #16, #29) cap at 16 for the same
  reason — the brief names the obvious liquidation pun as the thing to avoid.
- The preachy ones (#17, #18) lose on laugh-not-tear: a trader at 3am does
  not want a lesson, and the judge's brief says "never lectured".
- #15 and #31 fail criterion 1 outright (no loss in them) — they lose to
  any fortune that names the loss, per the brief.
- #1 beats #2 on freshness and tightness: #2's "holding it all along" is
  the same double meaning (fortune = money + slip) but stated flatly, with
  one extra word and a weaker last word ("along" vs "freshly baked").
